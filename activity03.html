Activity 3: BJT Curve & Load-Line Explorer

The transistor’s curves show what it can do; the load line shows what the circuit lets it do. The Q-point sits where they agree. Drive the base and slide that point from cutoff, up through the active region, and into saturation, then predict your Tinkercad readings before you build.

💡 What to notice
With RB = 470 kΩ, drag β and the Q-point moves: in the active region the transistor’s gain sets IC. Switch to RB = 10 kΩ and drag β again: the Q-point barely budges. A saturated switch doesn’t care what β is, because the collector resistor rules. That indifference is why switching circuits are designed to saturate: they work the same with any transistor from the parts bin. The load line’s ends never move unless you change RC or VCC; the transistor must always operate somewhere on that line.

Guided explorations

Use “Set it up” to load the starting conditions, then mark each one done.

  1. Walk through the three regionsWith RB = 100 kΩ, sweep VBB from 0 to 5 V. Record the VBB where IC first appears and the VBB where the region label flips to SATURATION.
  2. β matters in the active regionAt RB = 470 kΩ, drag β from 50 to 300. The base current stays fixed, yet the Q-point slides along the load line. Note IC at β = 50 and β = 300.
  3. β doesn’t matter in saturationAt RB = 10 kΩ, drag β across its full range. Explain why IC hardly changes, and why IC/IB here is not β.
  4. Move the load lineKeep RB = 470 kΩ and β = 300. Step RC through 470 Ω, 1 kΩ, and 2.2 kΩ. Which value pushes the same base drive into saturation, and why?
  5. The PNP mirrorSwitch to PNP at the Saturated preset. The curves are the same, but every polarity in the readout flips. State the turn-on condition for each type in terms of base versus emitter voltage.

VCE is the referee

  • ≈ VCC: cutoff, open switch.
  • In between: active, IC ≈ β·IB.
  • ≈ 0.1–0.2 V: saturation, closed switch.
  • Measure β only in the active region.
