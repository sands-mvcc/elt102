Activity 1: Diode I-V Curves & Optical Isolation

Two simulators, one idea: a P-N junction controls current one way and turns current into light. Start by mapping a diode's full characteristic curve, then send a signal across an optocoupler's isolation barrier and try to break it.

⏱ About 45 minutes 🧪 Part A: I-V curve grapher 💡 Part B: optocoupler simulator ✅ Check off each exploration as you finish it

📈 Part A: Interactive Diode I-V Curve Grapher

Source: Lesson 1, Semiconductor Fundamentals & Diodes

The curve is plotted live from the Shockley diode equation plus a modeled breakdown region. Drag Applied Voltage to move the green operating point and watch the diode switch between OFF, ON, and BREAKDOWN. Then raise the temperature and watch the knee slide left while leakage grows.

ID = IS ( eVD / (n·VT) − 1 )    VT = kT/q ≈ 26 mV at 25 °C

Guided explorations

Use “Set it up” to load the starting conditions, then mark each one done.

  1. Find the kneeWith Silicon selected, sweep VD upward and note where current first reaches 1 mA. Compare it with the “Knee @ 1 mA” value in the readout.
  2. Temperature driftPark VD at 0.65 V, then slide temperature from 25 °C to 100 °C. Current climbs sharply even though voltage never changed: the origin of thermal runaway.
  3. Compare materialsSwitch between Silicon and Germanium and record how far left the knee moves.
  4. Zener wallSelect the Zener preset and sweep VD toward −6 V. Watch the operating point hit the near-vertical regulation region around −5 V.

Reading the curve

  • Forward: near-zero current, then an exponential rise past the knee.
  • Reverse: a flat line of leakage that roughly doubles every 10 °C.
  • Breakdown: a near-vertical plunge at the breakdown voltage.
  • VF falls about 2 mV per °C.

💡 Part B: Interactive Optocoupler Isolation Simulator

Source: Lesson 2, Diode Applications & Optoelectronics

Your input signal drives the infrared LED (watch it glow with current), light crosses the isolation barrier, and the phototransistor rebuilds the signal on a completely separate circuit. Inject common-mode noise onto the input-side ground: it shakes the input trace, yet the isolated output stays clean.

CTR = (IC / IF) × 100%   →   IC(out) = IF(LED) × CTR
Input signal
🎯 What to notice
Output current is IF × CTR, pulled up through a 1 kΩ load. When that product drops below about 4.5 mA, the output can no longer reach a clean logic HIGH and the readout flags WEAK COUPLING, the same failure you see in an aged optocoupler.

Guided explorations

Use “Set it up” to load the starting conditions, then mark each one done.

  1. Light tracks currentDrag LED drive from 2 mA to 20 mA and watch the LED glow and the output amplitude grow together. Calculate IC by hand at 8 mA and check the readout.
  2. Test the barrierTurn on common-mode noise. Describe what happens to the input lane versus the isolated output lane, and explain why light can't carry the noise.
  3. Age the partHold IF at 10 mA and lower CTR until the output turns red. Record the CTR where coupling fails, then find the minimum IF that recovers it.
  4. Send real dataSwitch to Data pulses and pause the display. Read the bit pattern from the output lane and confirm it matches the input lane.

Design rules

  • Input resistor: RIN = (VLOGIC − VF) / IF.
  • Design with the minimum datasheet CTR; it drops as the LED ages.
  • Keep input and output grounds separate, or the isolation is gone.
  • Typical isolation: 2.5–5 kV.
